Working from home became common during the pandemic, and many companies have kept the arrangement since then. Some employees prefer it, while others are keen to return to the office. In my view, the office still suits most people better, although home working has clear benefits.

The strongest argument for working from home is the time it saves. In large cities, a daily commute can take two hours, and that time is unpaid. Employees who work from home can start earlier, finish earlier and spend the evening with their families. They also save money on transport and lunches, which matters for younger staff on lower salaries. A further benefit is that people with small children or elderly parents can arrange their day around caring responsibilities, so they stay in jobs they might otherwise leave.

However, an office offers something that a video call cannot reproduce. New employees learn by watching experienced colleagues, asking quick questions and overhearing how problems are solved. This kind of training is difficult to arrange online, and it is often the reason why young graduates struggle in their first remote job. Teamwork also suffers, because misunderstandings that would be settled in a five-minute conversation can take days by message.

In conclusion, home working saves time and money, but the office remains the better place to learn and to build a team. A mixed arrangement, with two or three days in the office each week, is probably the practical answer for most companies, and it allows staff to keep the benefits of both.
